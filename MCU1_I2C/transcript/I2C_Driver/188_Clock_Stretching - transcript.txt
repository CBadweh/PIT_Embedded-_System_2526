Clock stretching is one of the most powerful picture of I2C protocol. Clock stretching simply

means that holding the clock to 0 or ground level. The moment clock is held at low, then the whole

I2C interface pauses until clock is given up to its normal operation level.

So, what is the use of clock stretching? I2C devices, either master or slave, uses this feature to

slow down communication by stretching SCL or clock at low, which actually prevents the clock to rise

high again and the I2C communication stops for a while. In an I2C communication the master

device determines the clock speed. Isn't it?

However, there are situation where

an I2C slave is not able to co-operate with the clock speed given by the master and needs to slow

down a little.

In that cases, slave takes the advantage of clock stretching and by holding the clock at ground

level or 0, it momentarily pauses the I2C communication.

Take a example here. In the first figure, it's a no clock stretched scenario where slave is able to cope

up with the master. First, slave gets the data from the master up to 8th clock cycle and slave sends the

ACK back in the 9th clock cycle, which is perfect.

But, take a look at the second figure.

Here, master has sent a byte of data which is still 8th clock cycle.

But, since slave is busy in doing something else it doesn't let the clock to rise high again

over here. Instead it stretched the clock to low. If clock stretching wasn't enabled here, than the 9th clock

cycle would

have trigger here by the master and master would have interpreted that as a NACK, because the SDA is

held high.

So, this is slaves way of telling master that I am busy to send out the ACK.

Please, hold down. Then slave releases the clock here and sends out the ACK here, which is perfectly fine.

So, here I2C

interface is paused momentarily due to clocks stretching. And remember you need not to control the

clock stretching and unstretching by your code. The I2C hardware does it for you automatically.

You only need to enable the clock stretching feature,
