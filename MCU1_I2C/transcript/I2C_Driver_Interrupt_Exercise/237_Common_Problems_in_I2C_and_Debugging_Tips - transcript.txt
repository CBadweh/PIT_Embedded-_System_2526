Now let's discuss debugging ACK failure.

This is one of the most common problems in I2C communication.

The problem can't be solved by just staring at the code, but by probing the signals on the logic analyzer.

Most people just say that I2C is not working when they don't get the expected behavior. But when we

probe the signals,

this is what we see in the case of ACK failure. Here you can see that master has generated start condition

followed by the address phase but for that, there is no ACK,

that means there is a NACK. So, master firmware couldn't able to proceed further.

So, when you get this error of ACK failure here, then think about what are the possible ways in which this

could happen.

The first one could be generating the address phase with the wrong slave address.

If the address is wrong, then slave will not respond to this address phase.

As a result, you'll get a NACK here.

So, go and verify the slave address again.

Second reason could be not enabling the ACKing feature in the slave configuration register.

It is worth to check the I2C control register to see whether it is really enabled or not.
